# 小学英语情景表达与实用写作任务库

资料性质：原创学习补充材料，不对应某一出版社或教材版本。
适用年级：小学三至六年级。
检索关键词：English speaking, dialogue, invitation, email, note, directions, shopping, asking for help, practical writing, 情景表达。

## 1. Asking for help politely

Useful expressions:

- Excuse me. Could you help me, please?
- Could you say that again, please?
- I don't understand this word. What does it mean?
- Thank you for your help.

Dialogue:
A: Excuse me. Could you help me find the science room?
B: Sure. Go upstairs and turn left. It is next to the library.
A: Thank you very much.
B: You're welcome.

Polite requests often use could, can or please. “Give me your ruler” sounds like a command; “Could I use your ruler, please?” is more suitable between classmates.

## 2. Asking for and giving directions

- Where is the museum?
- How can I get to the museum?
- Go straight. Turn left/right at the crossing.
- It is opposite the bank / between the park and the library / next to the station.
- It is about ten minutes' walk from here.

Safety: Children should not follow a stranger to an unknown place. Ask a police officer, staff member or trusted adult, and use a map when possible.

## 3. Shopping

- Can I help you?
- I'd like this notebook, please.
- How much is it/are they?
- Do you have it in blue?
- May I try it on?
- Here is your change.

Dialogue:
Shop assistant: Can I help you?
Nina: Yes. I'd like two pencils and a notebook, please.
Shop assistant: That's six yuan.
Nina: Here you are.

Use “How many” for a number and “How much” for a price or an uncountable amount.

## 4. Making plans and invitations

Invitation essentials: activity, date, time, place and a way to reply.

Sample invitation:

Hi Leo,

Would you like to join our class book swap? It will be in the school library from 3:30 to 4:30 this Friday. Please bring one clean children's book. Let me know before Thursday.

From,
Chen Yu

Accepting: Thank you. I'd love to come.
Declining politely: Thank you for inviting me, but I have a music lesson then. I hope you have a great time.

## 5. Writing a short email

Structure:

1. Greeting: Dear Anna, / Hi Sam,
2. Reason for writing.
3. Important details in a clear order.
4. Closing: Best wishes, / See you soon,
5. Name.

Sample:

Dear Anna,

Our class visited the city science museum yesterday. My favourite part was the space room because I could move a model robot arm. I also learned why astronauts need special suits. What is your favourite science topic?

Best wishes,
Mei

Do not include a home address, phone number, password or other private information in a classroom writing task unless a trusted adult and the task specifically require safe handling.

## 6. Writing a notice

A notice should be short and easy to scan.

LOST

I lost a blue pencil case in the reading room on Tuesday afternoon. It has a small yellow star on it. If you find it, please give it to the school office.

Li Ming, Class 4

The description should help identify the object, but it should not reveal unnecessary personal information.

## 7. Describing a process

Use sequence words: first, next, then, after that, finally.

Example: How to make a paper seed pot

First, fold a sheet of old newspaper in half. Next, wrap it around a small cup. Then fold the extra paper under the cup. After that, remove the cup carefully. Finally, add soil and a seed.

Use command verbs for instructions: fold, cut, put, mix, wait. Add safety warnings when tools or heat are involved.

## 8. Comparing choices

Useful frame:

I prefer ___ because ___. ___ is ___ than ___, but ___. It is a better choice for me because ___.

Example:
I prefer walking to school because my home is nearby. Walking is slower than taking a car, but it gives me exercise and does not need fuel. I walk with an adult and use the crossing.

## 9. Speaking repair strategies

When communication breaks down:

- Sorry, I didn't hear you.
- Do you mean …?
- Let me try again.
- How do you say “……” in English?
- Could you spell that, please?

Good communication is not speaking without mistakes. It is noticing a problem and finding a polite way to make the meaning clear.

## 10. Practice tasks

1. Rewrite politely: Open the window.
2. Ask the price of two toy cars.
3. Give directions: the library is across from the park.
4. Write a two-sentence reply accepting a Saturday picnic invitation.
5. Write a polite reply declining because you will visit your grandparents.
6. Add missing information: “Come to our game next week.”
7. Put in order: finally / first / then / next.
8. Write a lost-item notice for a green water bottle without giving private contact details.
9. Correct the email closing: “Dear Ben, … Dear Wang Hui”.
10. Improve this sentence with a reason: “I like the reading club.”

## 11. Sample answers

1. Could you open the window, please?
2. How much are the two toy cars?
3. The library is across from the park.
4. Thank you for inviting me. I'd love to join the picnic on Saturday.
5. Thank you for the invitation, but I will visit my grandparents that day. I hope you have a lovely time.
6. Add the exact date, time, place, activity and reply method.
7. first, next, then, finally.
8. LOST: I lost a green water bottle near the playground after lunch. It has a white rabbit sticker. Please give it to the school office if you find it.
9. Begin with Dear Ben, and end with a closing such as Best wishes, followed by Wang Hui.
10. I like the reading club because we share interesting books and learn from one another.

Final check: Is the purpose clear? Are the time and place exact? Is the tone polite? Are verbs and punctuation correct? Did I avoid unnecessary private information?
